Swiss Baseline Template

A new Swiss Style document template, grounded in sourced research rather than in the look of the style.

Contents

    Session Objective

    Design and ship a Swiss Style / International Typographic Style document template alongside the existing bauhaus-baseline, focused on grid systems as they apply to web design, minimalism and clarity. The template had to be grounded in canonical sources rather than in a description of what Swiss Style looks like — the working hypothesis being that the movement's contribution is a method (one centre, radii at module multiples, hierarchy by size and weight) and not a surface (Helvetica, red, white space).

    Key Decisions

    • A sibling baseline template, not a change to the core design system. The Bauhaus template set the precedent: a standalone artefact in docs/baseline/ plus a draft source, with no edits to skills/floreo/SKILL.md. Registering Swiss as a core template would require re-baselining the SkillSpector fingerprints — a larger change than the work justifies.
    • Kept the core token vocabulary and changed only the values. --ca, --cab, --cw-bg, --cg-bg, --f-h, --s1–--s8 and the .pg / .sc / .note / .tbl class names all stay as documented, so the template passes the validator and stays compatible with the contract in QUICK_REFERENCE.md. The Bauhaus template diverges from this and fails the validator on six counts; Swiss deliberately does not.
    • The grid is visible but faint. The column overlay sits at 2.2% ink. At 5% it read as stripes rather than as structure — the intent is that you feel the grid, not read it.
    • Callouts became rules. The core system's tinted callouts are a deliberate anti-Bootstrap choice, so they do not travel into a Swiss template; callouts are a 2px top rule with an uppercase label, and the tint tokens are spent on diff rows and table highlighting instead.
    • Stated the palette doctrine as "sparing colour", not "one red". The sources say sparing; the document says so, and then uses red as the worked example. The heading keeps the memorable phrasing, the prose corrects it.
    • Sourced the poster from the artefact rather than from memory. Fetching the 1955 Beethoven poster and reading the geometry off it corrected two things: the conductor was Carl Schuricht, not Karajan, and the poster is entirely monochrome — no red at all.
    • Dropped the Die gute Form details I had planned. They were wrong on all three counts: it was Max Bill, for the Swiss Werkbund, at the Mustermesse Basel and the Kunstgewerbemuseum Zürich — not a Kunsthalle exhibition, and not a Hüsemann curatorial. This is why the details were checked before being written down.

    Blockers & Open Questions

    Warning: two research sub-agents were killed mid-flight after they fanned out to sixteen sub-agents and hit rate limits. Two of their topics — Expo 67 Swiss graphics, and Swiss colour palettes by designer — never landed. Neither is load-bearing for the document, but a deeper canon pass would want them, and the fan-out should be capped next time.
    • Sources disagree on which face Zürich used. The Swiss government's history says Helvetica; Poster House says Akzidenz-Grotesk. Both agree on Basel and Univers. The document presents both readings and offers a reconciliation explicitly labelled as inference. Not resolvable from the sources consulted.
    • The book's numeric grid tables were not read. Only the 8-to-32 field range came from a publisher blurb. No specific millimetre gutters are asserted anywhere in the document, and that gap should stay visible rather than be filled from a secondary source.
    • agent-skill-effective-docs-63v remains open and is the only ready issue on the board.
